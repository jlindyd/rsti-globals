Reliability Transformation

From reactive to predictive.

A design-centric reliability model replaces the reactive one: Reactive → Preventive → Proactive → Predictive.

02 — Four Phases

  1. Phase 1 · 0–6 mo

    Assess & Stabilize

    Establish the baseline: audit current reliability practice and stabilize the failure points already known.

  2. Phase 2 · 6–12 mo

    Design-In Reliability

    Move reliability upstream, into the design decisions that determine it before a part is ever built.

  3. Phase 3 · 12–18 mo

    Predictive Health Management

    Instrument the fleet and the line so failure is anticipated rather than discovered.

  4. Phase 4 · 18–24 mo

    AI & Digital Twin Optimization

    Close the loop: a living model of the product and process that keeps improving itself.

03 — The Executive Scorecard

Five levels, each with a leading and a lagging indicator.

  1. 01

    Design-In Reliability

    How much reliability is decided at the design stage, before manufacturing ever sees the part.

  2. 02

    Manufacturing Reliability

    How stable the process holds under real, unavoidable variation on the line.

  3. 03

    Asset Reliability & Maintenance

    How well fielded assets are kept running, and how that maintenance is planned.

  4. 04

    Predictive Reliability (PHM + AI)

    How much failure is anticipated by data before it is reported by a customer.

  5. 05

    Continuous Improvement & Learning

    How field experience and prior failures feed back into the next design cycle.

Leading and lagging indicators are set per organization, against its own baseline — RSTI Globals helps define them in the Executive Workshop and the Enterprise Transformation program.
